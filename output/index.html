The most important part of a first underwater experience often happens
before anyone’s face touches the sea.

It happens on the edge of the marina, while a visitor is still deciding
whether nervousness is a warning or simply the body’s way of paying
attention. It happens during the briefing, when unfamiliar equipment
becomes a series of small, understandable objects: a mask, fins, a
harness, a regulator, and a hose leading to an air supply floating on
the surface. It happens when a guide answers the same question twice
without making the guest feel foolish for asking.

Sint Maarten is an island that invites quick plans. Cruise passengers
arrive with a limited day. Visitors staying on the island have beaches,
restaurants, viewpoints, and neighborhoods competing for attention. The
temptation is to compress every experience. Underwater, that instinct is
rarely helpful. The sea rewards a slower transition.

A practical first step is simply to arrive early. SNUBA SXM operates
from the marina side of Philipsburg, close enough to the cruise port for
a short walk, but island traffic and ship-day crowds can still change
the pace of a morning. Giving yourself extra time means the tour begins
with a conversation rather than a sprint.

The briefing is not a delay before the activity. It is the first part of
the activity. A good guide explains how to breathe through the
regulator, how to clear a mask if water enters, how to communicate
without words, and how to move without fighting the water. The guest
should know that there is time to pause and that comfort matters more
than keeping up with someone else.

SNUBA uses a surface-supported air system. The air supply stays on a
raft above, while a lightweight hose connects the participant’s
regulator to that supply. There is no scuba tank on the back and no
scuba certification is required for eligible beginners. Participants
remain in shallow water and close to their guide. Children age eight and
older may be eligible, although every participant should review current
requirements and answer medical questions honestly before joining.

Then comes the moment people imagine as a leap. In practice, it is
better treated as an ordinary breath.

Put your face in the water. Exhale. Inhale. Listen to the bubbles.
Notice that the regulator keeps providing air. Look at the guide. Stay
where you are until breathing feels less like a task. There is no prize
for descending quickly.

This is where individual differences become visible. One guest wants to
move forward almost immediately. Another needs several minutes at the
surface. A child may be curious but cautious. An adult who has spent
years insisting that they are “not a water person” may discover that the
hardest step was simply allowing enough time for the body to believe the
equipment.

Once the breathing rhythm settles, attention widens. The mask is no
longer the center of the experience. The shallow underwater route begins
to show itself: fish passing through, sunlight changing over sand and
reef growth, mooring structures becoming habitat, and other participants
moving in three dimensions rather than walking along a path.

Most guided SNUBA experiences in Sint Maarten take about two to two and
a half hours from welcome to goodbye, including briefing, practice, and
the underwater portion. The activity generally stays within a shallow
range of roughly 10 to 20 feet. Those numbers are useful for planning,
but they do not explain why a first underwater breath stays in memory.
The lasting part is the change from anticipation to ease.

Visitors should bring swimwear, a towel, drinking water, and
reef-conscious sun protection. They should also bring questions. Current
times and availability can change with operations and sea conditions, so
the direct SNUBA SXM booking page is the right place to check the live
calendar: https://snubasxm.com/book-snuba/

After the underwater portion, leave room for the experience to land. Dry
off, drink something, and listen to what each person noticed. One guest
may remember the sound of bubbles. Another may remember a fish that
hovered close. Someone else may be quietly proud of having done
something they had spent the morning considering backing out of.

That half-hour before the first underwater breath is not empty waiting
time. It is the bridge between imagining the sea and entering it. When
it is handled calmly, the first breath does not need to feel heroic. It
can feel normal – and that is often what makes the rest of the
experience possible.

Author disclosure: Michel Wouterse is the owner of SNUBA SXM and writes
from the perspective of a local underwater-tour operator.

About the author: Michel Wouterse has lived and worked in Sint Maarten’s
underwater tourism community since 2008. He is the owner of SNUBA SXM
and writes about first-time underwater experiences, small-group guiding,
and the practical details that help visitors enjoy the sea with
confidence.

The Coconut Island: क्यों कहलाता है ‘कोकोनट आइलैंड’

लक्षद्वीप को ‘द कोकोनट आइलैंड’ या नारियल द्वीप कहने के पीछे एक ठोस कारण है:

  • पेड़ों की विशाल संख्या: लक्षद्वीप के द्वीपों पर लगभग 26 लाख से अधिक नारियल के पेड़ हैं। यहां प्रति व्यक्ति नारियल के पेड़ों की संख्या भारत में सबसे अधिक है।

  • आजीविका और अर्थव्यवस्था: नारियल स्थानीय लोगों की आजीविका का मुख्य साधन है। यहां नारियल की रस्सी (कॉयर), तेल और सूखा नारियल (कोपरा) जैसे उत्पाद बनाए और निर्यात किए जाते हैं।

  • भोजन का अभिन्न अंग: स्थानीय भोजन नारियल के बिना अधूरा है। चाहे वह नारियल का दूध हो, कसा हुआ नारियल हो या नारियल का तेल, लगभग हर व्यंजन में इसका उपयोग होता है।

  • सांस्कृतिक महत्व: धार्मिक अनुष्ठानों से लेकर सामाजिक समारोहों तक, नारियल हर जगह उपस्थित रहता है।

The Coconut Island: लक्षद्वीप का नाम और इतिहास

  • पुराना नाम: 1973 से पहले इसे ‘लक्कादीव-मिनिकॉय-अमिनीदिवि’ के नाम से जाना जाता था। 1 नवंबर 1973 को इसका नाम बदलकर ‘लक्षद्वीप’ कर दिया गया।

  • शाब्दिक अर्थ: ‘लक्षद्वीप’ संस्कृत से लिया गया है, जिसका अर्थ है ‘एक लाख द्वीप’।

  • भूगोल: वास्तविकता में यहां 36 द्वीप हैं, जिनमें से केवल 10 द्वीपों पर लोग निवास करते हैं। यह भारत का सबसे छोटा केंद्र शासित प्रदेश है, जिसका क्षेत्रफल मात्र 32 वर्ग किलोमीटर है।

The Coconut Island: घूमने लायक प्रमुख द्वीप

  • कवरत्ती: लक्षद्वीप की राजधानी; यहां का सुंदर लैगून और समुद्री एक्वेरियम पर्यटकों को आकर्षित करता है।

  • अगाती: एकमात्र द्वीप जहां हवाई अड्डा है। इसका पानी इतना साफ है कि कई मीटर गहराई तक समुद्री जीवन देखा जा सकता है।

  • बंगारम: प्राकृतिक सुंदरता और कोरल रीफ्स के लिए प्रसिद्ध; यह हनीमून के लिए बेहद लोकप्रिय है।

  • कदमत: सफेद रेत के समुद्र तट और वॉटर स्पोर्ट्स की सुविधाओं के लिए उत्कृष्ट।

  • मिनीकॉय: दूसरा सबसे बड़ा द्वीप; यहां की संस्कृति मालदीव से प्रभावित है और यहां का लाइटहाउस प्रसिद्ध है।

The Coconut Island: रोमांचक गतिविधियां

  • स्कूबा डाइविंग और स्नॉर्कलिंग: क्रिस्टल साफ पानी में रंगीन कोरल रीफ्स और मछलियों को करीब से देखना।

  • कयाकिंग: शांत लैगून में पानी पर तैरते हुए द्वीप की सुंदरता का आनंद लेना।

  • ग्लास बॉटम बोट राइड: बिना पानी में उतरे नाव के तल में लगे कांच से समुद्र के नीचे का जीवन देखना।

From Honeymoon to Second Safari: Sarah and Andrew’s 27-Year Journey with Somak

📅 Jul 07, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

July 2026

We recently caught up with returning clients Sarah and Andrew, who first travelled with Somak 27 years ago for a Kenya safari and Mauritius honeymoon. Nearly three decades later, they came back to us for another adventure,  this time a Tanzania safari taking in Lake Manyara, the Serengeti and the Ngorongoro Crater, finishing with four nights at the stunning Gold Zanzibar Beach House & Spa. Here’s what they had to say.

Where did you travel with us 27 years ago and what made you book with us again?

27 years ago we booked a safari to Kenya followed by a week in Mauritius. Ash was so passionate about the business and how important it was for them to provide a first class, memorable experience for their clients. I just knew this was the perfect company to choose to provide a bespoke honeymoon. It was excellent, so memorable. Safari is by far the best holiday for us and we knew we wanted to go on another safari holiday. In particular we wanted to experience the Serengeti and Ngorongoro. There was no need to consider anyone other than Somak.

What was the single moment that made you think “this is why I came”?

It’s the moment where you realise you have stepped into something truly spectacular that is so far removed from normal life. It is a privilege to be seeing so many animals in their natural world in a beautiful country. Just truly magical.

How did this trip compare to any previous safari or beach holiday you’ve taken?

There was such a contrast between our different safari regions, all beautiful in different ways. The people of Tanzania are so open and warm. They genuinely love to share information about their culture and find out more about yours.

Who would you say this itinerary is perfect for?

I would say the itinerary is suited to those who want to have a varied safari experience with different areas and types of accommodation. Tented camps are not for everyone but for us, we wished we had more nights in a tent! Just fabulous hearing the animals close by at night when you are tucked up in your luxury tent. What a thrill!

What was your most memorable wildlife encounter across the three parks – Lake Manyara, Serengeti, and Ngorongoro?

There were so many memorable moments. Goose bumps all over when we spotted two lionesses on some rocks playing together. We were so close and the only truck in the area. Wonderful! We were mesmerised and could have stayed all day. We saw a pride of lions lying beside their fresh kill, bellies full, lounging in the sunshine. Also watching the stunning giraffes at golden hour. Perfect light for seeing such majestic animals. Breathtaking when you witness so many animals passing in front, to the side and behind the truck. Seeing so many animals together on the plains, zebra, wildebeest, Thomson’s gazelles, all doing their bit to help each other. Feast for the eyes, memories forever.

How seamless did the Somak logistics feel?

Perfectly seamless. Somak representatives were always there on time waiting to warmly greet and look after us.

Any tips you’d give someone booking this for the first time?

Be sure of the balance wanted between safari and beach. We were happy to have more safari and less beach. We didn’t mind long days travelling in the safari truck. It was a great way to see more of Tanzania and the way of life. Oscar was so informative on different tribes, how people live and different ways to earn their daily bread.

Do you have a favourite story or moment with Oscar, your driver guide, that really stuck with you?

Oscar is so experienced. Pole pole was the way to go and we, and he, saw so much more. A lot of the truck drivers race from one sighting to another and must miss so much. Oscar’s x-ray eyes spotted a lion in a tree on the way out of Ngorongoro that our camera only captured with a full zoom lens. Amazing way to finish the day.

Best Time to Visit Fiji: When to Go for the Perfect Fiji Holiday

 

A Fiji holiday represents the ultimate tropical escape. It is close enough for a direct flight from major hubs like Sydney, Melbourne, Brisbane, Canberra and Cairns, yet far enough to feel an ocean away from the daily grind. Deciding when to go to Fiji depends entirely on your personal travel style, budget, and holiday preferences. Understanding seasonal shifts helps you match your itinerary to the perfect island conditions.

Understanding Fiji’s Two Main Seasons

 

Fiji enjoys a beautiful tropical marine climate year-round, but its weather pattern is broadly divided into two distinct periods: the dry season and the wet season. Knowing how the Fiji weather shifts between these two ensures you pack correctly and set the right expectations for your trip. 

 

The Dry Season (May to October)

 

Considered the best time to visit Fiji for classic outdoor activities, the dry season brings lower humidity, minimal rainfall, and beautifully clear skies. Typical daytime temperatures hover around a comfortable 26°C to 27°C, dropping to a refreshing 19°C to 20°C at night. Steady trade winds keep the air moving, preventing that sticky, heavy feeling. Water temperatures remain highly inviting at around 25°C to 26°C, making it peak time for swimming and aquatic adventures. 

 

The Wet Season (November to April)

 

The wet season (or tropical summer) brings warmer temperatures (frequently reaching 30°C or higher) alongside increased humidity and tropical downpours. While it sounds intense, rain often falls heavily overnight or in brief afternoon bursts, leaving plenty of daylight sunshine to enjoy. Ocean water temperatures also peak at a balmy 28°C.

Cyclone Season Awareness

November to April is cyclone season in the South Pacific. While severe weather systems can occur, modern tracking is exceptional, and island resorts are fully equipped to handle tropical conditions safely. Fiji remains a vibrant, year-round destination with plenty of tropical warmth to offer even during these summer months.

 

The Shoulder Season Advantage: May and November

 

If you want the ultimate balance of good weather and excellent value, look closely at the shoulder months of May and November.

  • May: This beautiful transition month marks the beginning of the dry season. The landscapes are lush and vibrant after the summer rains, the trade winds are just picking up, and the heavy winter crowds have not yet arrived. It is a fantastic window to lock in flexible resort options or cheaper flight prices.
  • November: As the transition into the wet season, November brings a slight rise in humidity but rewards travellers with hot, sunny days, quieter resorts, and highly competitive package rates.

 

Your Travel Calendar According To Your Preference

 

Here is a breakdown of the best months to visit Fiji based on what matters most to your itinerary. 

 

Chasing Flawless Weather (July to September)

 

This is the absolute peak of the dry season. If your dream holiday involves clear blue skies, minimal rain, and low humidity, this is the time to go. It’s ideal for sun-seekers who want guaranteed pool and beach days. 

 

Beating the Crowds (February to March, May, October to November) 

 

Traveling outside of the main Australian school holiday windows means quieter beaches, empty resort pools, and a more relaxed island pace. You will get to experience the famous Fijian hospitality with a much more intimate feel. 

 

Scoring Cheaper Prices (February to March)

 

Travel demand drops during the wetter months, resulting in the lowest prices of the year. For budget-conscious travellers, this is the prime window to find incredible flight deals and heavily discounted resort stays.

 

Snorkelling and Diving (May to October)

 

The dry season brings optimal underwater visibility, sometimes exceeding 30 metres. Because there is minimal stormwater run-off during these months, the coral reefs are crystal clear, making it a dream period for exploring Fiji’s world-famous marine life.

 

A couple snorkelling off an island in Fiji. Photo courtesy of Tourism Fiji.

 

Surfing the Big Breaks (May to August)

 

Southern Hemisphere winter swells generate consistent breaks along the outer reefs. If you are looking to tackle iconic waves like Cloudbreak, this windy, high-swell window is your sweet spot. 

 

Hiking and Nature Adventures (May to June)

 

If you want to explore Fiji’s lush interior, national parks, and hidden waterfalls, aim for the start of the dry season. The cooler daily temperatures make trekking much more comfortable before the landscape dries out later in the year. 

 

Sunset hike in the Yasawa Islands, Fiji. Photo courtesy of Tourism Fiji.

 

Family Holidays (June to August, December to January)

 

These months align perfectly with major Australian school holiday periods. While the resorts are busier, this is when they ramp up their kids’ clubs, daily activities and family-focused entertainment programs to keep everyone happy. 

 

Regional Weather: How It Changes Across the Islands

 

Fiji’s geography means that weather conditions can vary quite a bit between the mainland of Viti Levu and the outer island groups. 

 

Denarau and Nadi

 

Located on the western side of the main island, this region is generally drier and sunnier than the eastern side. It is highly accessible and acts as a central hub for top-tier mainland resorts, making it a great choice for a smooth, low-stress stay. ,

 

The Mamanuca and Yasawa Islands

 

These outer island chains sit in a distinct microclimate that receives less annual rainfall than the mainland. If you are chasing picture-postcard imagery of uninterrupted sunshine, white sand beaches, and clear turquoise water, taking a short boat ride or seaplane flight out here is your best bet. 

 

The Coral Coast

 

Located on the southern coast of Viti Levu, this lush region features dramatic coastlines and receives slightly more rain, which keeps the rainforest vibrant and green. It is home to iconic beachfront properties that offer incredible cultural depth, like traditional fire-walking ceremonies, without requiring outer-island transfers.
