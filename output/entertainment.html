Best Time to Visit Fiji: When to Go for the Perfect Fiji Holiday

 

A Fiji holiday represents the ultimate tropical escape. It is close enough for a direct flight from major hubs like Sydney, Melbourne, Brisbane, Canberra and Cairns, yet far enough to feel an ocean away from the daily grind. If you’re planning a romantic getaway, a diving expedition, or browsing all-inclusive Fiji holiday packages for a family break, timing is everything.

Deciding when to go to Fiji depends entirely on your personal travel style, budget, and holiday preferences. Understanding seasonal shifts helps you match your itinerary to the perfect island conditions.

Fijian ladies performing a traditional Meke dance. Photo courtesy of Tourism Fiji.

Understanding Fiji’s Two Main Seasons

 

Fiji enjoys a beautiful tropical marine climate year-round, but its weather pattern is broadly divided into two distinct periods: the dry season and the wet season. Knowing how the Fiji weather shifts between these two ensures you pack correctly and set the right expectations for your trip. 

 

The Dry Season (May to October)

 

Considered the best time to visit Fiji for classic outdoor activities, the dry season brings lower humidity, minimal rainfall, and beautifully clear skies. Typical daytime temperatures hover around a comfortable 26°C to 27°C, dropping to a refreshing 19°C to 20°C at night. Steady trade winds keep the air moving, preventing that sticky, heavy feeling. Water temperatures remain highly inviting at around 25°C to 26°C, making it peak time for swimming and aquatic adventures. 

 

The Wet Season (November to April)

 

The wet season (or tropical summer) brings warmer temperatures (frequently reaching 30°C or higher) alongside increased humidity and tropical downpours. While it sounds intense, rain often falls heavily overnight or in brief afternoon bursts, leaving plenty of daylight sunshine to enjoy. Ocean water temperatures also peak at a balmy 28°C.

Cyclone Season Awareness

November to April is cyclone season in the South Pacific. While severe weather systems can occur, modern tracking is exceptional, and island resorts are fully equipped to handle tropical conditions safely. Fiji remains a vibrant, year-round destination with plenty of tropical warmth to offer even during these summer months.

 

The Shoulder Season Advantage: May and November

 

If you want the ultimate balance of good weather and excellent value, look closely at the shoulder months of May and November.

  • May: This beautiful transition month marks the beginning of the dry season. The landscapes are lush and vibrant after the summer rains, the trade winds are just picking up, and the heavy winter crowds have not yet arrived. It is a fantastic window to lock in flexible resort options or cheaper flight prices.
  • November: As the transition into the wet season, November brings a slight rise in humidity but rewards travellers with hot, sunny days, quieter resorts, and highly competitive package rates.

 

Your Travel Calendar According To Your Preference

 

Here is a breakdown of the best months to visit Fiji based on what matters most to your itinerary. 

 

Chasing Flawless Weather (July to September)

 

This is the absolute peak of the dry season. If your dream holiday involves clear blue skies, minimal rain, and low humidity, this is the time to go. It’s ideal for sun-seekers who want guaranteed pool and beach days. 

 

Beating the Crowds (February to March, May, October to November) 

 

Traveling outside of the main Australian school holiday windows means quieter beaches, empty resort pools, and a more relaxed island pace. You will get to experience the famous Fijian hospitality with a much more intimate feel. 

 

Scoring Cheaper Prices (February to March)

 

Travel demand drops during the wetter months, resulting in the lowest prices of the year. For budget-conscious travellers, this is the prime window to find incredible flight deals and heavily discounted resort stays.

 

Snorkelling and Diving (May to October)

 

The dry season brings optimal underwater visibility, sometimes exceeding 30 metres. Because there is minimal stormwater run-off during these months, the coral reefs are crystal clear, making it a dream period for exploring Fiji’s world-famous marine life.

 

A couple snorkelling off an island in Fiji. Photo courtesy of Tourism Fiji.

 

Surfing the Big Breaks (May to August)

 

Southern Hemisphere winter swells generate consistent breaks along the outer reefs. If you are looking to tackle iconic waves like Cloudbreak, this windy, high-swell window is your sweet spot. 

 

Hiking and Nature Adventures (May to June)

 

If you want to explore Fiji’s lush interior, national parks, and hidden waterfalls, aim for the start of the dry season. The cooler daily temperatures make trekking much more comfortable before the landscape dries out later in the year. 

 

Sunset hike in the Yasawa Islands, Fiji. Photo courtesy of Tourism Fiji.

 

Family Holidays (June to August, December to January)

 

These months align perfectly with major Australian school holiday periods. While the resorts are busier, this is when they ramp up their kids’ clubs, daily activities and family-focused entertainment programs to keep everyone happy. 

 

Regional Weather: How It Changes Across the Islands

 

Fiji’s geography means that weather conditions can vary quite a bit between the mainland of Viti Levu and the outer island groups. 

 

Denarau and Nadi

 

Located on the western side of the main island, this region is generally drier and sunnier than the eastern side. It is highly accessible and acts as a central hub for top-tier mainland resorts, making it a great choice for a smooth, low-stress stay. ,

 

The Mamanuca and Yasawa Islands

 

These outer island chains sit in a distinct microclimate that receives less annual rainfall than the mainland. If you are chasing picture-postcard imagery of uninterrupted sunshine, white sand beaches, and clear turquoise water, taking a short boat ride or seaplane flight out here is your best bet. 

 

The Coral Coast

 

Located on the southern coast of Viti Levu, this lush region features dramatic coastlines and receives slightly more rain, which keeps the rainforest vibrant and green. It is home to iconic beachfront properties that offer incredible cultural depth, like traditional fire-walking ceremonies, without requiring outer-island transfers.

Beyond the Beach: Vanuatu for the Adventurous

 

While many travellers imagine a South Pacific getaway as a series of quiet moments on a sun lounger, Vanuatu tells a different story. This archipelago of 83 islands serves as a premier playground for those who prefer their paradise with a side of adrenaline. Beyond the translucent lagoons and white sand, you will find active volcanoes, dense jungles, and a cultural heritage that remains vibrantly alive in everyday life.

Choosing the right Vanuatu holiday packages allows you to balance high-octane exploration with the comforts of a tropical retreat. The diversity of places to visit in Vanuatu ensures your itinerary remains exciting.

 

A couple swings on a rope at Blue Lagoon, Efate, Vanuatu

Thrilling Vanuatu Adventure Activities

 

Vanuatu is widely considered one of the most accessible places on earth to experience raw, geological power and untouched wilderness. If you are looking for Vanuatu activities that get the heart racing, these experiences should be at the top of your list.

 

The Power of Mount Yasur


Standing on the rim of an active volcano as it rumbles and spews molten rock is a bucket-list moment for most visitors. According to our guide to Tanna Island, Mount Yasur is one of the world’s most accessible active volcanoes. You can take a 4WD across the vast ash plains before a short hike leads you to the crater’s edge. The experience is particularly spectacular at twilight when the glowing lava illuminates the night sky, providing a thunderous sensory experience unlike any other.

 

Mt Yasur, Tanna Island, Vanuatu.

 

Blue Holes and Jungle Kayaking

 

In Espiritu Santo, adventure takes a more aquatic turn. The island is famous for its “Blue Holes”, natural freshwater springs filtered through limestone that glow with a blue hue. As noted in our guide to Santo, one of the best ways to experience these is by kayaking up a narrow, jungle-fringed river. The silent paddle through the rainforest ends in a crystal-clear swimming hole where you can swing from rope swings into the cool water.

 

Canopy Ziplining and Waterfall Treks

On the main island of Efate, you can trade the ground for the sky by ziplining through rainforest canopies, soaring over canyons with views stretching out to Mele Bay. For those who prefer a vertical challenge, Vanuatu adventure activities include trekking to the Mele Cascades, where you can abseil down the face of the waterfall or simply hike to the top for a panoramic view of the island’s lush interior.

 

The World’s Best Wreck Diving

Vanuatu’s maritime history and coral health make it a top-tier destination for divers. The “SS President Coolidge” off the coast of Santo is a world-renowned wreck dive, offering a labyrinth of holds and decks to explore. To help plan your aquatic itinerary, our Vanuatu travel guide provides more detail on the best seasonal conditions for visibility in 2026.

 

Diving at the shipwreck SS President Coolidge, Espiritu Santo, Vanuatu.

 

Unique Cultural Experiences in Vanuatu

 

The true soul of the islands lies in “Kastom”, the traditional culture and ancestral knowledge of the Ni-Vanuatu people. Engaging with local communities offers a meaningful way to understand how ancient traditions shape modern life.

 

Authentic Village Visits and Kastom Ceremonies

 

Many communities welcome visitors to learn about traditional gardening, weaving, and medicinal plants. These are genuine insights into a way of life that has remained unchanged for centuries.

 

A central pillar of unique cultural experiences in Vanuatu is the Kava ceremony. Kava is a sedative, non-alcoholic drink made from the crushed root of the Piper methysticum plant. In Vanuatu, kava is more than a beverage; it is a sacred social glue used to welcome guests, seal alliances, and resolve community disputes.

  • The Ritual: Traditionally, the root is pounded or ground into a fine pulp, mixed with water, and strained through coconut fibre. It is typically consumed in a Nakamal (a communal meeting house) at dusk.
  • The Experience: When offered a shell of kava, it is customary to drink the earthy, peppery liquid in one go. You will likely feel a gentle numbing of the lips and tongue followed by a deep sense of relaxation and mental clarity. It is a quiet, reflective ritual that encourages peaceful conversation rather than rowdy celebration.

Beyond the Nakamal, you will often find giant Banyan trees standing as the spiritual cathedrals of the forest. These massive, ancient trees with their sprawling aerial roots serve as sacred meeting places. Many villages are built around these giants, believing they protect the community. Witnessing a performance beneath the canopy of a Banyan tree, with the rhythmic thumping of feet on the volcanic soil, offers a profound connection to the land and its people that stays with you long after your Vanuatu holiday has ended.

 

The traditional ways of Ni-Vanuatu people.

 

Fire Dancing and Cultural Festivals 

 

Fire dancing is a spectacular display of skill and bravery often seen at night in Port Vila. Performers use rhythmic percussion and flaming torches to tell ancestral stories, creating a mesmerising spectacle against the dark ocean backdrop. Depending on the timing of your trip, you might also witness a “Naghol” (land diving) ceremony on Pentecost Island, the precursor to modern bungee jumping, which is one of the most famous Vanuatu cultural activities.

 

Pentecost Island Land Diving Ceremony

 

The Vibrant Port Vila Markets 

 

For a taste of daily life, visit the Port Vila Markets. Here, you can browse stalls filled with organic produce, hand-woven baskets, and local delicacies like the national dish: laplap, a paste of yams, banana, breadfruit and other local fruits that is wrapped in banana leaves and cooked in an underground oven with coconut cream.
Exploring the markets is one of the essential things to see in Port Vila for anyone wanting to support local artisans and taste the authentic flavours of the islands.

The best way to experience what to do in Vanuatu is to secure a central base that offers all-inclusive convenience. Staying at an all-inclusive resort means that after a day of volcano trekking or wreck diving, your meals, drinks, and evening entertainment are already taken care of, allowing you to focus entirely on the adventure.

Their all-inclusive packages often bundle daily meals and beverages, which is a massive advantage when you are returning from a long day of Vanuatu adventure activities. The resort also offers its own set of experiences, from snorkeling on the house reef to private island dining.

Warwick Le Lagon Resort & Spa offers extensive all-inclusive options that simplify your holiday budget. With its own overwater activities and easy access to local village tours, it bridges the gap between relaxation and discovery. 

 

Practical Tips for Visiting Vanuatu

 

Deciding things to do in Vanuatu often depends on your appetite for the unknown. When planning your trip, keep these practicalities in mind:

  1. Respect the Kastom: Always ask permission before entering a village or photographing locals.
  2. Dress Appropriately: While swimwear is fine for the resort pool, modest clothing is expected when visiting local villages or markets.
  3. The All-Inclusive Advantage: Bundling your meals and transfers into your Vanuatu holiday packages saves time and prevents unexpected costs in remote areas.
  4. Pack for the Terrain: If you plan on trekking or visiting Mount Yasur, sturdy enclosed shoes are a necessity.

The Ultimate Palawan Island Hopping Guide

📅 Mar 09, 2025  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

So you’re dreaming of crystal-clear water, hidden lagoons, and beaches that look like they’ve been ripped straight from a postcard? Welcome to Palawan! —the Philippines’ crown jewel and the ultimate playground for island hopping. Whether you’re a solo adventurer, a couple chasing sunsets, a family hunting for kid-friendly fun, or an overseas Filipino craving a taste of home, this guide is your golden ticket. Let’s dive in!

What is Island Hopping? (And Why Palawan Does It Best)

Island hopping is exactly what it sounds like: jumping from one island to another by boat, snorkeling, swimming, and soaking up the scenery. Think of it like a pub crawl, but instead of bars, you’re hitting pristine beaches, secret coves, and neon-blue lagoons. Palawan takes this to the next level. Imagine Hawaii’s drama meets the Maldives’ serenity, with a dash of Jurassic Park vibes (minus the dinosaurs).

Must-See Spots You Can’t Miss:

  • Big Lagoon (El Nido): Kayak through emerald waters surrounded by limestone cliffs. It’s like paddling through a fantasy novel.
  • Kayangan Lake (Coron): Often called the “cleanest lake in Asia.” The hike up is steep, but the view? Chef’s kiss.
  • Hidden Beach (El Nido): Accessible only by swimming through a narrow crack in the rocks. Adventure level: Indiana Jones.
  • Siete Pecados (Coron): Snorkel above coral gardens and WWII shipwrecks. Perfect for history buffs and mermaid wannabes.
  • Port Barton: A quieter alternative to El Nido. Great for families who want to dodge the crowds.

Pro tip: If you’ve been to the Caribbean or Thailand’s islands, Palawan’s lagoons will still blow your mind. It’s that unique.

Best Island Hopping Tours in El Nido & Coron

Palawan’s two main hubs—El Nido and Coron—are like siblings with different personalities. El Nido is the wild, adventurous one. Coron is the laid-back, history-loving sibling. Here’s how to tackle both:

El Nido’s Famous “Tours A-D”:

  • Tour A: The classic. Hits Big Lagoon, Secret Lagoon, and Shimizu Island. Ideal for first-timers.
  • Tour C: For drama queens. Helicopter Island (yes, it looks like one) and hidden beaches.
  • Tour B: Caves and snorkeling spots. Great for families—easy to mix relaxation and exploration.

Coron’s Top Tours:

  • Super Ultimate Tour: A full-day blitz of Kayangan Lake, Twin Lagoon, and shipwreck sites. Bring your GoPro.
  • Island Escapade Tour: Chill vibes on Malcapuya and Banana Islands. Think powdery sand and coconut shakes.

For Overseas Filipinos: Some tours include stops at local villages. Perfect for reconnecting with Filipino culture—maybe even practice your Tagalog with the boat crew!

How to Book the Right Island Hopping Package

Booking a tour in Palawan isn’t rocket science, but here’s how to avoid “tourist trap” vibes:

  1. Read Reviews: Check TripAdvisor or Google. Look for phrases like “small group” or “eco-friendly.”
  2. Ask About Inclusions: Does lunch come with grilled seafood? Are snorkel gear and life jackets included?
  3. Private vs. Group: Couples might love a private boat for romance. Solo travelers? Join a group to make friends.
  4. Book Early (or Not): Peak season (Dec–May) sells out fast. But if you’re flexible, haggle with local operators last-minute for deals.

Pro Tip: Filipino tour guides often crack jokes and play upbeat OPM (Original Pilipino Music) on the boat. If you’re an overseas Filipino, lean into the nostalgia—it’s a vibe.

Budget vs. Luxury: Island Hopping for Every Wallet

Budget-Friendly ($20–$50/day):

  • Join a group tour. You’ll share the boat with 10–15 people, but hey, it’s social!
  • Pack your own snacks and water.
  • Stay in hostels or guesthouses. Try Mad Monkey Hostel in El Nido for a party scene.

Luxury Splurge ($200+/day):

  • Charter a private yacht. Yes, it’s a thing. Sail to VIP-only spots like Matinloc Shrine.
  • Stay at resorts like El Nido’s Pangulasian Island. Think infinity pools and butler service.
  • Add extras: Sunset champagne toasts, private beach picnics.

Families: Mid-range tours strike a balance. Look for operators with kid-sized life jackets and shaded boats.

Do You Need a Tour Guide? Solo vs. Group Trips

Solo Travelers: Guides are gold. They’ll handle permits, dodge sketchy weather, and share local legends (ask about the “hidden treasure” myths in Coron). Plus, you’ll bond with other travelers.

Couples: Want privacy? Skip the guide and rent a kayak or small boat. Just double-check safety gear first.

Families: Guides are lifesavers. They’ll help with kids, carry gear, and know which spots have restrooms (crucial intel!).

Overseas Filipinos: Guides can introduce you to off-menu experiences, like a lunch stop at a local karinderia (eatery) for adobo and rice.

What to Pack for Island Hopping in Palawan

Your backpack essentials:

  • Reef-Safe Sunscreen: Palawan’s coral reefs are fragile. Brands like Stream2Sea work great.
  • Dry Bag: Because phones + saltwater = bad.
  • Cash: Many islands don’t take cards. Stock up on pesos for fresh coconuts and souvenirs.
  • Water Shoes: Rocky beaches = ouch.
  • Reusable Water Bottle: Stay hydrated without trashing the planet.

For Families: Extra towels, kiddie sunscreen, and waterproof bandaids (rocks happen).

Pro Tip: Pack a sarong. It’s a towel, blanket, and sunshade all in one.

When to Go: Timing Your Trip Perfectly

Palawan’s dry season (November–May) is prime. December–April is busy but magical. Monsoon season (June–October) means cheaper deals but rougher seas. If you hate crowds, aim for November or May.

Local Eats to Fuel Your Adventure

Don’t miss:

  • Lato Salad: Sea grapes that pop in your mouth.
  • Fresh Tuna BBQ: Served on the boat.
  • Halo-Halo: Shaved ice dessert. Perfect after a swim.

For Overseas Filipinos: Tell your guide you crave sinigang or lechon. They’ll know where to go.

Final Thoughts: Your Palawan Adventure Awaits

Palawan isn’t just a destination—it’s a mood. Whether you’re kayaking through lagoons, laughing with new friends on a boat, or watching your kids hunt for seashells, these moments stick with you. So grab that sunscreen, book that tour, and get ready to fall in love with the islands.

Shangri-La Yanuca Island Fiji ~ Coral Coast

The activity coordinators at Little Chief’s Club entertain children with fun activities throughout their stay. It offers a range of supervised activities, allowing parents to enjoy some time to themselves while ensuring their children are well taken care of. Many of the activities take place at the children’s pool, on the beach, or at the children’s playground. Children aged under four require a babysitter.

Registration is at 9.30am daily.

Plantation Island Resort ~ Mamanuca Islands

Kids of all ages will be swept away by the holiday magic offered by Plantation Island Resort’s Coconut Kids Club. Each day offers a range of activities to keep your kids occupied and having fun from 9am – 8:30pm. Better still, there’s activities to ensure even the shyest of kids have a blast. With offerings ranging from traditional crafts such as coconut boat making and meke preparation to tie dye, beach games, and evening movies, your kids’ time in Fiji will be their best holiday ever. They may even find their new best friend! Three complimentary sessions are offered each day from 9.00am-12.30pm, 2.00pm-4.30pm, & 6.00pm-8.30pm.

Plantation Island also offers the Lairo Club for kids aged 12 to 16 years who are too young for adult activities and too old for kids activities. The club offers the group the idea to make new friends and compete at their own age level when it comes to activities. It also makes it easy for the age group to relate to each other.

Outrigger Fiji Beach Resort ~ Coral Coast

Ready for fun?! The Outrigger Fiji Beach Resort welcomes younger guests to a full day of supervised activities and tours. From water polo to an afternoon of fun Fijian games, they’ll be kept busy all day! And best of all, kids club activities are free to guests!

Little Riggers is run for 3 – 7 years, and the Beach Riggers program caters for 8 – 12 years.
Registration are from 9 am – 10 am. Activities are run from 10am – 12noon, then lunch to 12.30pm, followed by more activities 2-4pm. After a short break, dinner is then served from 5.30-6pm, followed by evening activities until 9pm.

Teens aren’t forgotten either! Activities for teens include weaving lessons, culture walk, spear-throwing, pool volleyball, teen bingo game and beach bonfire. Also included are interactive, fun, educational programs to help save our oceans, such as coral planting and building a fish house. All teen activities are free of charge.

The Outrigger Fiji also offers a MeiMei nanny service which is custom-tailored to your child’s needs and available for children 6 months – 12 years. Charges apply.

The Pacific island paradise of Fiji offers holidaymakers a huge choice of activities and experiences, meaning every day of your getaway can be filled with as little or as much as takes your fancy.

If working on your tan by a spectacular resort pool with a cool drink in hand is your thing, Fiji has you covered at many world-class hotels and resorts, set in lush surrounds. There are few destinations in the world better placed than Fiji to deliver a classic island flop and drop (and eat and drink) escape, filled with massages, leisurely meals, and hours in a hammock with a good book.

But Fiji offers so much more for couples and families looking for a fun, varied and memorable holiday.

If you’re keen to get the muscles moving there are adrenalin-pumping jet boat rides, quad bike tours, snorkelling adventures and beautifully maintained golf courses. Perhaps you’d like to meet locals and learn about their way of life, shop at local markets or visit a wildlife sanctuary?

Or swim with manta rays, relax in therapeutic mud pools or visit a beach club?

Fiji is perfectly positioned to deliver a holiday experience like no other, and caters to people travelling on all budgets, whether you’re looking for a basic room or five-star luxe treatment with all the trimmings.

Here is our checklist for some of the top things to do in Fiji. You may not squeeze them all in, but we reckon you’ll have a great time trying!

1. Kula Wild Adventure Park

The biggest, most diverse family fun park in Fiji is home to a nature walk through acres of natural forest with elevated boardwalks allowing exploration, from the valley floor to the forest canopy; Fiji’s only Roller-Coaster Zip Rail and Jungle Waterslide; and spectacular local bird, reptile and marine life. The 28-acre property is set in three valleys, with 12 acres of coastal forest open to visitors. Huge walk-through enclosures help put visitors inside the habitats of the resident parrots, doves, water birds and reptiles. Marine displays of ocean turtles, soft and hard coral and tropical fish provide a glimpse into the sensational marine world that surrounds the more than 300 islands of Fiji. Open seven days a week from 10am. Last entry at 3.30pm.

Location: Coral Coast. 

2. Sigatoka Sand Dunes

The Sigatoka Sand Dunes National Park highlights Fiji’s natural beauty. The sandy expanse of hills and dips is a spectacular landscape to view, and an awesome place to stretch your legs. You can visit the dunes on your own, or with a walking tour, which will generally take around one hour. Just be sure that if it’s a hot day you visit before 11am or after 3pm.

Location: Coral Coast.

3. Sigatoka River Safari

The Sigatoka River is the longest river on the main island of Viti Levu, running from the hills of the Navosa Province down to the sand dunes in Kulukulu, on the famous Coral Coast. The river provides locals with fresh water mussels (kai), fish, prawns and eels, and water for agriculture and drinking.

You’ll be collected from your resort in a mini-coach and transferred to Sigatoka Town. From there you’ll enjoy a 17km scenic cruise into the Sigatoka valley, discovering the fertile area known as the “salad bowl” of Fiji.

After arriving at your departure point, surrounded by Fijian natural beauty, you begin your journey up the river, hearing stories about the history of the region. The water is so clear that the fish and rocks are visible.

A warm welcome from villagers awaits when you reach your destination. Sigatoka River Safari visits a different village every day to minimise the impact of tourism.

You will visit the village Chief’s bure (house) or community hall and take part in a kava ceremony, before a tour of the village and a traditional Fijian lunch, including wonderful fresh tropical fruit.

Location: Coral Coast.

4. Cruises from Denarau Island

Is there anything more enjoyable than relaxing on the deck of a ship as you sail through tropical waters? Companies offer day trips to one of the nearby islands, each displaying an aspect of Fiji’s unspoilt beauty. Or enjoy a sunset dinner cruise with that special someone. Love is in the air, everywhere I look around…

Location: Denarau Island, about 10km from Nadi.

5. Golf

Denarau Island is home to a world-class 18-hole championship golf course, with an added tropical touch — its sand bunkers are carved into the shape of sea creatures. There are water hazards on 15 of the 18 holes, so you need to bring your A-game. And if you can’t bring your A-game, bring plenty of balls!

Location: Denarau Island.

6. Visit a beach club!

A visit to a beach club in Fiji is the ultimate escape, offering a blend of relaxation and adventure in a stunning setting. One of our favourites is Malamala Beach Club located on its very own island,  just 30 minutes from Port Denarau. Grab a full or half day pass and enjoy white sand beaches, cabanas & an infinity edge pool. Enjoy delicious food & sip on island cocktails. The perfect way to spend a few hours relaxing, and the photo opportunities are next level!

Location: Daily departures from Port Denarau.

7. Visit a local bar with a view and watch the sunset while sipping an icy cocktail

Nothing says beach holiday like cool, colourful cocktails and a liquid gold sunset. Never will the term “no filter” be more relevant, and let’s be honest, you can’t visit Fiji without at least one Insta to make your friends back home turn green with envy. Fiji is home to numerous bars and restaurants and many have signature cocktails made from the finest liquors and fresh, local ingredients.

Location: Wherever the wind takes you.

8. Treat yourself and indulge in a Fijian Bobo message

The traditional Fijian Bobo massage has been passed down through the generations. It’s a firm massage using a rich coconut oil blend to increase circulation, remove toxins and ease muscular aches and pains. Most Bobo massages apply warm poultices of medicinal herbs (such as Makita and Macou) to your main areas of tension. This powerful massage will put you in a trance and you’ll leave feeling lighter than ever.

Location: All over Fiji, in many of the resorts.

9. Check the children into a kids’ club and savour some adult time

We know you love your kids, but we also know you love some downtime. Most resorts in Fiji offer a Kids’ Club providing endless entertainment for your young ones. They’ll be in good hands, and it’ll provide you with the perfect opportunity to do a little bit of exploring on your own, or with that special someone. Just because you’re travelling with your family doesn’t mean romance goes totally out the door.

Location: Most Fijian resorts. Let us know if the Kids’ Club option is a priority when you’re booking accommodation.

10. Learn to snorkel or dive

Fiji is home to some of the Pacific Ocean’s clearest waters and attracts experienced snorkellers and divers from around the world to experience the striking reefs and exotic wildlife. What better place to take a class in snorkelling or diving? Several places in Fiji offer PADI courses in scuba diving (as well as Bubble Maker, the kid-friendly version). The best part is that once you’re certified you can take your certificate on any future holiday, as it’s internationally recognised. Dive into a course on your Fijian holiday.

Location: Guests at the famous Jean-Michel Cousteau Resort will have the option, otherwise check out Dive Wananavu Fiji in Rakiraki.

11. Swim with manta rays

The manta rays glide through the channel in the Yasawa Islands on the change of tide to feed. These strange and majestic creatures are natural performers and are mesmerising to behold. Curious and friendly, the manta rays are nothing to be scared of and swimming with them is a unique and wonderful experience. As one of the most pristine tropical marine environments on Earth, you’ll find a host of underwater life, from exotic fish to giant turtles. Lovers of nature or animals should make this priority number one.

Location: There are several islands in the Yasawas that do tours. Available May to October.

12. Learn about local culture

No-one will blame you if you’re on holiday to kick back and enjoy the weather, but for those a little more curious about the culture and tradition of Fiji, there are plenty of opportunities to hang out with the locals. Fijians are famously hospitable, and local schools host visits from travellers. Many villages across Fiji are more than happy to invite you to a kava ceremony. Most Fijians speak English so don’t be shy about saying hello. When you do, you’ll find out the locals’ reputation for friendliness is well-deserved.

Location: The Outrigger Fiji Beach Resort in Viti Levu offers community tourism programs, while most resorts in Fiji host kava ceremonies. If there’s a local village you wish to visit, you’ll find you’ll always be welcomed so long as you’re respectful.

Pro tip: It’s considered impolite to wear a hat around a village, and you should always leave your shoes outside the door when you enter a home.

13. Visit the Port Denarau Shopping Centre

You’ll find quality shops and dining located on the water’s edge at Port Denarau. It also offers a spa, hair and nail treatments and tour operators selling land, sea and air activities suitable for all age groups.

The port boasts mouth-watering cuisine and relaxed drinks at a range of restaurants and bars, plus there is a money exchange and clothing outlets.

Unwind on the sunny deck or enjoy an enchanted evening with local entertainment and a gorgeous, shimmering backdrop.

Location: Denarau Island.

14. Check out Cloud 9

Cloud 9 is Fiji’s only two-level floating platform and features a fully stocked bar and wood fire pizza hub surrounded by pristine turquoise waters. Stopping by Cloud 9 is a necessity, simply so at some point in the future you can say “remember the time we ate pizza at a floating bar above a reef nestled amongst those tropical islands?” But bragging rights are the least of what Cloud 9 provides. The venue regularly hosts international DJs and is a hub for music lovers — for a sample check out the Cloud 9 SoundCloud. Offering the most chilled vibes in Fiji, and an experience you’ll get nowhere else in the world, Cloud 9 is the perfect round trip.

Location: Daily departures from Port Denarau.

15. Make a day trip to Tivua Island and explore its 500 acres of beautiful reef

Tivua Island is one of the more secluded areas of Fiji, and a cruise out to this hidden gem is a great way to spend a day. The island has cultural activities for the kids to take part in, 500 acres of flawless coral gardens for the divers, and for everyone else, there are pristine beaches and views to die for. You can also take a dinner cruise, where you literally sail into the sunset. Add a barbecue dinner and some Fijian style entertainment and you spell out perfection.

Location: A variety of cruises depart morning, afternoon and early evening from Port Denarau Marina. Prices vary depending on the length of cruise and inclusives.

16. Visit Sabeto Hot Springs

Because of the tropical climate, there is a wet season in Fiji. Swimming in the rain is fun, but floating in a hot pool in the rain is just divine. The hot springs also offer therapeutic mud pools which kick the relaxation factor up to another level. Just remember, even if it’s raining in paradise, you’re still in paradise.

Location: Sabeto, approx 20 minutes from Nadi Airport. 

17. Hike through Fiji’s unique topography

From lush tropical rainforests and dramatic mountains to serene beaches and lagoons, Fiji’s vistas are breathtaking. If you like to do a bit of exploring on holiday there are a number of hiking trails for you to consider. Whether you’re with little ones or looking for a solo challenge, Fiji has something to offer.

Location: We recommend the Colo-i-Suva Forest Park, in Nasinu; the Lavena Coastal Walk, Taveuni Island; or the Vidawa Forest Walk, also on Taveuni Island.

18. Get spiritual

Depending on the island you decide to visit, many different retreats are available. The majority are designed to increase energy levels by improving mental and physical clarity. Such retreats are led by skilled yogis and natural health practitioners, and they’re a great way to relax and unwind or do some soul searching. Alternately, visit the Sri Siva Subramaniya Swami, the largest Hindu temple in the Southern Hemisphere, and one of the few Hindu temples outside India to feature ancient Dravidian architecture. It’s a vibrant and intricately decorated place of worship and is a great spot for photos, but just make sure you’re respectful — you can take pictures on the grounds, but refrain from taking them inside.

Location: The Sri Siva Subramaniya Swami is in Nadi, while yoga options are available across the islands.

19. Find yourself a quiet place with a hammock and get lost in a good book

For all the adventure and activities, the culture and cuisine, and the flora and fauna, sometimes nothing compares to kicking back. It’s your holiday, and putting your feet up and getting lost in a world of fact or fiction is just as valid as any other. If escaping the grind of daily life is what you’re looking for, you’ll find it at one of the many beautiful beaches or luxury resorts of this island nation.

Location: Anywhere you drop your towel.

20. Food glorious food

Eat the seafood, the fruit, and virtually anything else that can or will be put on a plate. Fijian cuisine is an experience unto itself so try as much as you can!

Location: When it comes to food, Fiji is your oyster. Speaking of oysters, eat them too. From the markets to the fanciest restaurants on the island, culinary sensations are everywhere.
